Privacy policy

Checkpoint Alerts maps sobriety checkpoints that law enforcement agencies have publicly announced. This policy describes what the app does with information, which is very little, because it is built not to have any.

What the app collects

Nothing. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no server of its own. Nothing you do in the app is transmitted to us, because there is no “us” to transmit to — the app is a viewer over public data.

Location

The app uses your location in exactly two places, both optional:

  • The locate button centers the map on where you are. Your position is read once, used to move the map, and discarded.
  • Nearby alerts, if you turn them on, compare your position against the checkpoints in the app's data when you open the app. The comparison happens on your phone. Your position is discarded when it finishes.

Your location is never stored, never logged, and never leaves your phone. Both features work only while the app is open, and both stop the moment you revoke the permission in system settings.

Preferences

Watched counties, the history toggle, alert settings, and a record of which alerts have already fired are stored on your phone and nowhere else. Uninstalling the app deletes them.

Notifications

Alerts are local notifications scheduled on your phone from data the app already has. There is no push service.

The map itself

The map is provided by your platform (Apple Maps on iOS, Google Maps on Android). Map tiles load through your operating system, which is governed by Apple's or Google's own privacy policy, the same as any app that shows a map. The website's map loads tiles from OpenStreetMap.

The checkpoint data

Every checkpoint in the app comes from a public announcement by the agency that ran it. None of it is user-reported, and none of it describes app users. The app refreshes that data by downloading a public file; the request carries no account, no identifier, and nothing about you.

Changes

If this policy changes, the change will be visible here. It cannot quietly become more invasive: a policy this short has nowhere to hide.
